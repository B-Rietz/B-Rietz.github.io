    The problem

    The work was there. Finding it was the job.

    A consultant's day is follow-ups, callbacks, and filling in what's missing on a lead or an opportunity. Those pieces lived in different places, so knowing what to do next meant clicking through records and screens to piece it together.

    It showed up everywhere. Consultants clicked through record after record to work out what was due. Follow-ups slipped. To-do lists lived outside the CRM. Key details like move-in timeline or financing stayed blank because filling them in took too many steps.

    For Lennar, that added up to three costs: selling time spent searching instead of selling, work that happened where managers couldn't see it, and a pipeline missing the details needed to forecast and coach.

    500+consultants and sales managers in the sales org I designed for
    Key decisions

    What I chose, and why

    1

    One prioritized list, not another dashboard

    I pulled tasks and missing lead and opportunity information into a single queue that's already sorted. The consultant doesn't have to decide where to look, only whether to do the top thing.

    2

    Single-select queue + contextual detail panel

    The list stays put on the left and the selected item's context opens on the right. You never lose your place, and you never leave the page to finish something.

    3

    Finish in one or two taps, then auto-advance

    Every item type has its fastest possible completion: one tap to mark done, one tap to pick an answer, or type-and-save for a missing field. Finishing an item moves you straight to the next one, so momentum carries.

    4

    Lower-priority work gets its own tab

    Rather than building elaborate filters, I moved low-priority items to a "Later" tab. The main queue stays short enough to feel finishable.

    5

    Progress you can feel

    A big goal for the project was gamification: building momentum into the day. I used gauges because they read at a glance, with a red-to-green completion gauge for today and a persistent weekly points gauge. I added banners that celebrate new points the moment you earn them, along with divisional leaderboards and incentive milestones.

    Process

    Prototype in code, so the feedback is real

    I built it as a working Lightning Web Components prototype instead of a mockup. People could click through real flows, so feedback was about the workflow rather than the picture of it.

    Most feedback centered on two things: which fields to show for each kind of item, and how records should be prioritized behind the scenes. Both shaped what the queue shows and the order it shows it in. That's exactly the kind of feedback a static mockup can't get you.

    Outcome: every feature worked end to end. Demo feedback from consultants and managers was positive, leadership greenlit it, and the team is now building it for production. Once it launches, I'd measure the share of tasks finished inside Salesforce, how many leads have move-in timeline and financing filled in, and time to first follow-up.
